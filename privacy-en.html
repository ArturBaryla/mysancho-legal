3. What we do not collect

3.1. We do not collect your location, contacts, call history, list of installed apps or the contents of your gallery. You choose a photo with your phone’s system picker, which gives the app only the file you pick.

3.2. We do not see your card number or bank account — payment is processed by the store.

3.3. We have no ads, analytics or profiling, and we do not sell data.

4. Who can see your data

4.1. Members of your groups see: your tag and profile photo; your Revolut tag, if you entered it; the expenses you added with amounts, descriptions, dates and receipt photos, the receipt reading and its split; your balance in the group.

4.2. Members of your groups do not see your e-mail address, purchases, subscription, token balance or any change history.

4.3. Other users can find your tag with the search (after typing at least its first three characters) to invite you to a group. You always choose your tag yourself — we never create it from your e-mail address or any other data. When someone invites you, you see their tag and the group’s name before joining. Nothing else is visible to people outside your groups.

4.4. A person without an account is visible only to the members of their group. They cannot be found with the search, invited, reported or blocked, and we send them no messages.

4.5. Photos have no permanent public address. The app downloads them through a link valid for 1 hour; during that time anyone who has the link can download the photo without signing in. Do not pass such links on.

4.6. Before you upload a photo, think about what else is in it. All members of the group see a receipt photo, including those who join later. Do not photograph it together with documents, cards or account numbers. A receipt may also reveal information about health (e.g. medicines from a pharmacy) or other very personal matters. Do not scan such receipts or add their photos if you do not want the group members to see them and the scanning provider to receive them.

4.7. Reports, blocks and the change log: only the app administrator and the system that assesses reports (point 5.5) see a report, and the author is not told who reported — the system does not receive the reporter’s details; only you see your blocks; only the administrator and that system read the change log, to find out who changed a reported item.

4.8. The app administrator has technical access to the database and photo storage, needed to run the service, and does not look at content except to fix failures, handle reports and meet legal obligations.

6. Purposes and legal bases

6.1. Performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) — account, groups, invitations and tag search, expenses, receipt reading and balances; purchases and tokens, including the e-mails about a purchase, a subscription renewal, a refund and auto-renewal being switched off, the welcome after you create an account (information about your starting tokens, no advertising), the confirmation that an account was deleted and the e-mail about cancelling a subscription after an account is blocked; a copy of data on your phone for working offline; push notifications about events in your groups — a service you switch on and off in your phone’s settings and in the app (Profile → Notifications).

6.2. Legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR) — records of settlements with the stores (Google, Apple) for tax and accounting; replying to data protection requests and complaints (Article 7a of the Polish Consumer Rights Act); confirming a contract of purchase on a durable medium, that is by e-mail (Article 21(1) of the Polish Consumer Rights Act); handling notices about content and giving reasons for moderation decisions (Articles 16 and 17 of the Digital Services Act).

6.3. Push notifications. We store the device token when your phone’s system allows the app to send notifications (on newer phones — after you allow it in the system dialog; on Android 12 and older, notifications are on by default). If you turn them off in the phone, we delete the token the next time the app starts.

6.4. Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR):

  1. the name of a person without an account — so the group can settle up with them,
  2. server logs, the IP address at sign-up and rate-limit refusals — security, diagnosing failures, protection against overload and mass account creation,
  3. scan counters — protection against abuse of a paid feature,
  4. blocks, warnings, suspensions and the change log — protecting users from content and behaviour that break the Terms,
  5. correspondence — handling matters and defending against claims,
  6. expenses, splits and receipt photos that stay in a group after you leave it or delete your account — so that the other members’ settlements stay correct,
  7. purchase data linked to the account — handling refunds and complaints, and defending against claims,
  8. the version and date of accepting the Terms — proof of the contract and defence against claims,
  9. consent to a purchase — proof that, before buying, you acknowledged that the Tokens are added to your Account at once and that from then on you lose the right to withdraw from the contract (Terms, section 14), and defence against claims. This is a consent under consumer law, not a GDPR consent — the basis for processing is our legitimate interest.

6.5. To create an account we need an e-mail address (or a Google or Apple account) and a tag — without them we cannot conclude the contract. A profile photo, a Revolut tag, receipt photos and notifications are optional.

6.6. We make no decisions based solely on automated processing that have legal effects on you (Art. 22 GDPR). The model reading a receipt only fills in fields that you can always correct.

8. Data on your phone

8.1. The app keeps a copy of your groups’ data on your phone so that it works offline, and a cache of photos.

8.2. This copy is not included in backups — neither Google’s on Android nor iCloud and Finder on an iPhone.

8.3. When you sign out, the app removes it from the phone.

9. Deleting your account

9.1. You delete your account in the app (Profile → Delete account) or by e-mail — write to the address in point 1.2 from the e-mail address of the account; we carry out such requests within 30 days.

9.2. Deleted at once: e-mail address (from the account; a copy needed only to send you the confirmation that the account was deleted is removed once it is sent, after 24 hours at the latest); tag (replaced with an ID that carries no data); profile photo (if it was reported, the copy stays with the report — section 7); Revolut tag; language; device tokens, notification settings and muted groups; the sign-in account.

9.3. Stays in your groups: the expenses, splits, settlements and receipt photos you added; people without an account you added; your ID in the change log (at most 180 days). We detach them from your account and e-mail address and replace you with an ID that carries no data. This is not full anonymisation: group members may remember who added an expense, and descriptions and photos may point to you. You can object or ask us to delete particular entries (point 9.6).

9.4. Stays outside your groups (under the rules in section 7): reports, warnings and suspensions concerning the account, and purchase data, consents to purchases and purchase confirmations under an ID that carries no data (together with the store’s purchase ID). RevenueCat keeps a customer record with that ID — at your request we will delete it there too.

9.5. Deleting your account does not cancel your subscription. Cancel it in Google Play (Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions) or on an iPhone (Settings → your name → Subscriptions), otherwise you will keep being charged. Unused tokens are lost together with the account.

9.6. Why expenses stay: they are part of other people’s shared settlement — deleting them would break the balances of people who had nothing to do with your decision. If you want them deleted as well, write to us — we will look at it individually and tell you how it would affect the others’ settlements.

10. Your rights

10.1. You have the right to:

  1. access your data and get a copy of it,
  2. rectify your data,
  3. have your data erased,
  4. restrict processing,
  5. data portability,
  6. withdraw consent, where processing is based on it — at any time, without affecting the lawfulness of processing before the withdrawal.

10.2. Right to object. You may at any time object — on grounds relating to your particular situation — to the processing of your data based on our legitimate interests (point 6.4). We will then stop processing it unless we show compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or grounds for establishing, exercising or defending legal claims.

10.3. Write to the address in point 1.2 — we reply within one month. For a complex request we may extend this by two months — we will tell you within the first month.

10.4. You can lodge a complaint with the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes Urzędu Ochrony Danych Osobowych, ul. Stawki 2, 00-193 Warsaw, Poland) or with the supervisory authority in the country where you live.

10.5. A person added to a group without an account has the same rights. We have no address or other contact for them, so we cannot inform them directly (Art. 14(5)(b) GDPR) — we publish this information here, and the Terms require the person adding them to let them know. If someone added you like that and you do not want it, write to us with the group’s name and the name or nickname you were added under — we will change or remove the entry. If we cannot tell which entry is yours, we will tell you so (Art. 11(2) GDPR); the group admin can also change or remove it on the “Members” screen.

10.6. Decisions taken automatically (Art. 22 GDPR). Reports made in the App are assessed automatically by a system based on artificial intelligence, and its decision may remove your content, warn you, suspend or block your Account — a decision that significantly affects you. The basis is Art. 22(2)(a) GDPR (the decision is necessary for performing the contract for using the App and the rules of the terms), and the duty to give reasons and allow an appeal follows from Articles 17 and 20 of the Digital Services Act. You always receive the reasons (what, why, which point of the terms). You have the right to human intervention, to express your point of view and to challenge the decision: write to the address in point 1.2 within 60 days — a person will review the appeal within 14 days (terms, point 17.6). The system decides only when it is sure of its decision; doubtful cases go to a person, and blocking an Account permanently requires particularly high confidence. For this purpose we do not profile you and do not assess your personal characteristics beyond what is in the reported content and in the history of breaches on the Account.

11.3. If you are a parent or guardian and believe that a child below this age has given us personal data, write to the address in point 1.2 — we will delete it.
